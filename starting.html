Starting Your Experience

Start Your Engaged Learning Experience

Once you have prepared yourself and your team, the next step is to build a strong working relationship with your partner. The beginning of an engaged learning experience sets the foundation for communication, trust, collaboration, and project success.

ELO provides resources to help you communicate professionally, plan productive first meetings, clarify roles, and formalize expectations with your partner.

Why the Beginning Matters

First impressions can shape the relationship between your team and your project partner. Clear communication, thoughtful preparation, and realistic expectations can help establish a strong foundation from the beginning.

Your first interactions are an opportunity to clarify what everyone understands about the project. They also provide space to identify questions, discuss roles, establish communication expectations, and make sure the project scope is understood by everyone involved.

Early ambiguity is not necessarily a problem. Learning how to work through uncertainty, ask questions, and develop shared expectations is an important professional skill.

Phase 2: Starting Your Experience

The starting phase focuses on establishing professional communication, conducting productive initial meetings, and creating clear agreements between students, teams, and project partners.

The goal is to move from preparation into action while making sure everyone understands the purpose, boundaries, responsibilities, and expectations of the project.

What to Focus On

Partner Communication

Develop professional communication habits when reaching out to your project partner. Be clear, respectful, organized, and mindful of the partner's time.

Establish expectations for communication, including preferred channels, meeting schedules, and response times.

First Meetings

Use your first meeting to establish relationships, understand the project, clarify roles, and discuss the boundaries of the work.

A well-planned agenda can help your team make the most of the meeting while leaving room for questions and conversation.

Formalizing Agreements

Document important commitments, responsibilities, communication expectations, and project scope so that students and partners have a shared understanding of the work.

Before Your First Meeting

Take time to prepare as a team before meeting with your partner. A little preparation can make it easier to ask useful questions and listen carefully during the meeting.

Questions to discuss with your team

  • What do we already understand about the project?
  • What questions do we still have?
  • What do we know about our partner's goals?
  • What responsibilities will each team member have?
  • What information do we need from the partner?
  • What parts of the project scope are still unclear?
  • What communication questions should we address?
  • What outcomes would indicate that the project is moving in the right direction?

Communicating with Your Partner

Professional communication helps establish trust and makes it easier for students and partners to work together. Your first message should provide enough information for your partner to understand who you are, why you are contacting them, and what you hope to accomplish.

Before sending your first message, check that it:

  • Clearly introduces you and your team.
  • Identifies the purpose of the project or experience.
  • Uses a professional and respectful tone.
  • Makes the requested next step clear.
  • Provides relevant scheduling information.
  • Gives the partner an opportunity to ask questions.

Remember that communication is a two-way process. Listen carefully to your partner and avoid assuming that your team's initial understanding of the project is complete.

Make Your First Meeting Productive

The first meeting is an opportunity to establish a relationship while also developing a shared understanding of the project.

A useful first meeting can include the following steps:

  1. Introduce the team. Make sure everyone knows who is involved and what each person will contribute.
  2. Learn about the partner. Give the partner space to explain their organization, community, goals, and needs.
  3. Discuss the project. Review the current understanding of the project's purpose, goals, and scope.
  4. Clarify expectations. Discuss responsibilities, deadlines, deliverables, and other important commitments.
  5. Discuss communication. Decide how often the team and partner will communicate and which channels should be used.
  6. Identify open questions. Record questions or uncertainties that need to be resolved later.
  7. Agree on next steps. End the meeting with clear actions, owners, and timelines.

First Meeting Checklist

Use this checklist to make sure your team addresses the most important topics during the kickoff.

  • Introductions have been completed.
  • The partner has explained their goals and needs.
  • The team has explained its understanding of the project.
  • Roles and responsibilities have been discussed.
  • Project scope and boundaries have been discussed.
  • Important deadlines have been identified.
  • Communication preferences have been discussed.
  • Questions and uncertainties have been documented.
  • Next steps have been identified.

Build Trust and Psychological Safety

Starting a project is also about establishing the relationship that will support the work. Teams need to create an environment where people can ask questions, share concerns, and communicate honestly.

Psychological safety and trust can make it easier to discuss uncertainty and address problems before they become larger issues.

Ways to support a strong partnership

  • Listen actively to your partner.
  • Ask questions instead of making assumptions.
  • Be honest about what your team does and does not know.
  • Follow through on commitments.
  • Communicate when plans or timelines change.
  • Respect your partner's expertise and experience.
  • Create space for different perspectives.
  • Address misunderstandings early.

Clarify the Project Scope

Project scope can change as students and partners develop a deeper understanding of the work. At the beginning, focus on creating a shared understanding of what the team is trying to accomplish.

Discuss:

  • The primary goals of the project.
  • The intended audience or users.
  • Expected deliverables.
  • Important deadlines and milestones.
  • Team and partner responsibilities.
  • Available resources and constraints.
  • What is outside the project's scope.

Having these conversations early can help prevent misunderstandings and provide a reference point when project needs change later.

Formalize Your Partnership

Once expectations have been discussed, document important agreements. Written agreements give students and partners a shared reference point throughout the experience.

Depending on your project, this may include information about roles, communication, responsibilities, timelines, project scope, and other commitments.

A Quick Guide to Getting Started

  1. Prepare your team and review what you already know.
  2. Contact your partner professionally.
  3. Schedule and prepare for the first meeting.
  4. Listen carefully to your partner's goals and needs.
  5. Clarify roles, responsibilities, and project scope.
  6. Establish communication expectations.
  7. Document agreements and next steps.
  8. Begin the project with an understanding that your plans may evolve as you learn more.

Ready to Begin the Work?

A strong start gives your team a foundation for the work ahead. As the project develops, new information, constraints, feedback, and challenges may require your team to adapt.

Keep communicating with your partner, track your progress, and be prepared to revisit your original plans when necessary.

The next phase focuses on managing and executing your project while conducting research, communicating progress, and responding to feedback.
